Consider the following diagram, we notice that the boost converter can be broken down into 2 simple RLC circuit:

![[Pasted image 20250727173118.png]]


## Switch in position 1

Inductor voltage (using KVL on the left loop) and capacitor current (opposite to load current):
$$
\begin{align}
v_L &= V_g \\
i_C &= -\frac{v(t)}{R}
\end{align}
$$
Using the small ripple approximation:
$$
\begin{align}
v_L &= V_g \\
i_c &\approx -\frac{V}{R}
\end{align}
$$

## Switch in position 2

Inductor voltage (using KVL on the left loop) and capacitor current (opposite to load current):
$$
\begin{align}
v_L &= V_g - v(t)\\
i_C &= i_L - \frac{v(t)}{R}
\end{align}
$$
Using the small ripple approximation:
$$
\begin{align}
v_L &= V_g - V \\
i_c &\approx I_L - \frac{V}{R}
\end{align}
$$

1. By the volt-second balance, if the voltage in the first interval is positive, the voltage in the second interval must be negative.
2. By the capacitor charge balance, the same applies for the capacitor current

# Output Voltage

![[Pasted image 20250727175737.png]]

1. Integrating inductor voltage over 1 switching period
2. Apply **volt-second balance** (avg inductor voltage = 0)
3. Solve for V

$$
\begin{align}
\langle v_L \rangle &= \frac{1}{T_s} \int_{0}^{T_s} v_L(t) dt \\
\langle v_L \rangle &= \frac{1}{T_s} \cdot (D T_s)(V_g) + (D' T_s)(V_g - V)  \\
0 &= \frac{1}{T_s} \cdot (D T_s)(V_g) + (D' T_s)(V_g - V)  \\
V &= \frac{V_g}{D'} = \frac{V_g}{1-D}
\end{align}
$$

## Voltage conversion ratio

$$
M(D) = \frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} = \frac{V}{V_g} = \frac{1}{1-D}
$$
![[Pasted image 20250727175134.png]]
Plotting the functions shows that as duty cycle increases, the output voltage get multiplied exponentially

# Inductor current (DC component)

![[Pasted image 20250727175809.png]]

1. Integrating capacitor current over 1 switching period
2. Apply **capacitor charge balance** (avg capacitor charge = 0)
3. Solve for I

$$
\begin{align}
\langle i_L \rangle &= \int_{0}^{T_s} i_C(t) dt \\
0 &= (-\frac{V}{R})(DT_s) + (I - \frac{V}{R})(D'T_s) \\
I_L &= \frac{V}{D'R}
\end{align}
$$
We know the output voltage (derived above) is:
$$
V = \frac{V_g}{D'}
$$
Combining the 2 equations we can express $I_L$ in terms of $V_g$:
$$
I = \frac{V_g}{D'^2 R}
$$

# Inductor current (Ripple)

First we find inductor current when switch is at position 1:

1. We know that $v_L = V_g$ (derived at the beginning),
2. We then use inductor formula to get the slope
$$
\begin{align}
v_L(t) &= L \frac{di_L(t)}{dt} \\
\frac{di_L(t)}{dt} &= \frac{v_L(t)}{L} \\
\frac{di_L(t)}{dt} &\approx \frac{V_g}{L}
\end{align}
$$
The same applies to position 2:

$$
\begin{align}
v_L(t) &= L \frac{di_L(t)}{dt} \\
\frac{di_L(t)}{dt} &= \frac{v_L(t)}{L} \\
\frac{di_L(t)}{dt} &\approx \frac{V_g - V}{L}
\end{align}
$$

Knowing this we can then plot the waveform

![[Pasted image 20250727180852.png]]

Note that the change in inductor current is defied as above (half of pk-pk ripple)

To calculate for pk-pk ripple, we:
1. Multiply the slope by time passed to find the height
2. Rearrange to find $\Delta_L$
$$
\begin{align}
2 \Delta i_L &= \frac{V_g}{L} \cdot (DT_s) \\
\Delta i_L &= \frac{V_g}{2L} \cdot (DT_s)
\end{align}
$$

# Capacitor Voltage Ripple

1. Use capacitor formula
2. Rearrange to solve for slope
3. $i_C(t)$ is derived at the beginning
4. Repeat for when switch is at position 2

Switch at position 1
$$
\begin{align}
i_C(t) &= C\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} \\
\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} &= \frac{i_C(t)}{C} \\
\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} &= \frac{-V}{R} \cdot \frac{1}{C} \\
\end{align}
$$
Switch at position 2
$$
\begin{align}
i_C(t) &= C\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} \\
\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} &= \frac{i_C(t)}{C} \\
\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} &= \frac{I}{C} - \frac{V}{RC} \\
\end{align}
$$

We can then plot the output voltage

![[Pasted image 20250727182904.png]]

$$
\begin{align}
2 \Delta i_L &= \frac{-V_g}{RC} \cdot (DT_s) \\
\Delta i_L &= \frac{V}{2RC} \cdot (DT_s)
\end{align}
$$
