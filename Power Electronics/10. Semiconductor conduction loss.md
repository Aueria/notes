# Applying the transformer model

![[Pasted image 20250803203034.png]]

1. This circuit can be simplified by summing all resistors and voltage together (connected in series)
2. After that, we can then push it through the transformer so solve it

The output voltage is:
$$
V = \bigg( \frac{1}{D'} \bigg) (V_g - D'V_D) \bigg( \frac{D'^2R}{D'^2R + R_L + DR_{on} + D'R_D} \bigg)
$$
The conversion ratio is calculated by dividing the whole equation by $V_g$:
$$
\frac{V}{V_g} = \bigg( \frac{1}{D'} \bigg)  \bigg( 1 - \frac{D'V_D}{V_g} \bigg)  \left( \frac{1}{1 + \cfrac{R_L + DR_{on} + D'R_D}{D'^2R}} \right)
$$

# Efficiency

We use the same method in notes 8:
1. Find $P_{in}$ and $P_{out}$
$$
\begin{align}
P_{in} &= (V_g)(I) \\
P_{out} &= (V)(D'I)
\end{align}
$$
Then, efficiency is calculated by:
$$
\eta = \frac{P_{in}}{P_{out}} = \frac{(V_g)(I)}{(V)(D'I)} = \frac{V}{V_g}D'
$$
By rearranging, we get the solution:
$$
\eta = \frac{V}{V_g}D' = \cfrac{\left( 1 - \cfrac{D'V_D}{V_g} \right)}{ \left(1 + \cfrac{R_L + DR_{on} + D'R_D}{D'^2R} \right)}
$$
From the final equation (efficiency), we can see that 2 conditions are needed for high efficiency:
$$
\begin{align}
V_g/D' &>> V_D \\
D'^2R &>> R_L + DR_{on} + D'R_L
\end{align}
$$
