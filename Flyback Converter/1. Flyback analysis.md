Advantages:
1. Simple to design

3. Low parts count
4. Multiple outputs

Disadvantages:
1. Low power (Typically <100W)
2. Needs output load. Voltage will keep climbing and break the output filter capacitors

# Basics of PWM

Most Flybacks converter use PWM control

![[Pasted image 20250811182124.png]]

$V_{avg}$ can be calculated by:
$$
\begin{align}
V_{avg} &= \frac{1}{T_p} \int_0^{T_p} v(t) \, dt \\ 
&= \frac{1}{T_p} \int_0^{T_{on}} V_{pk} \, dt + \frac{1}{T_p} \int_{T_{on}}^{T_p} 0 \, dt \\
&= \frac{V_{pk} \cdot T_{on}}{T_p} + \frac{0 \cdot (T_p - T_{on})}{T_p} \\
&= \frac{T_{on} \cdot V_{pk}}{T_p} \\
&= D \cdot V_{pk}
\end{align}
$$
Where D is duty cycle

## Analysis

1. Draw schematic for $T_{on}$
2. Draw schematic for $T_{off}$
3. Solve both circuit

![[Pasted image 20250811192310.png]]

### On
$$
\begin{align}
V_{Lp} &= V_{in} \\
T_{on} &= D \cdot T_p \\
i_{Lp} &= \frac{V_{in}}{L} \cdot T_{on}
\end{align}
$$
Current in primary side acts like a ramp
### Off
$$
\begin{align}
V_{Lp} &= -\frac{V_0}{N} \\
T_{on} &= (1 - D) \cdot T_p \\
i_{Lp} &= 0
\end{align}
$$
Current in primary side is 0 as switch is off
### Waveform

![[Pasted image 20250811192947.png]]

Applying volt second rule (avg voltage of inductor is 0):
$$
V_{in} \cdot D \cdot T_p - \frac{V_o}{N} (1-D) T_p = 0
$$
Solving for $V_o$ gives
$$
V_o = \frac{N \cdot D}{1 - D} \cdot V_{in}
$$
# Design Example

Specifications:
1. $V_{in}$ = 24V - 34V
2. $Vo$ = 12V @ 0.5A
3. Frequency 100kHz

Assumptions:
1. Duty cycle (D) = 0.5
2. Efficiency = 100%

>[!info]
>This does not follow the usual equations in most books
>Instead, we first calculate the voltage and current at the secondary and transfer to the primary. Finally calculating the primary inductance of the flyback

## 1. Calculate output power

Output power
$$
P_o = 12V \cdot 0.5A = 6W
$$
Assuming 100% efficiency, input power = output power
$$
P_{in} = P_o = 6W
$$

## 2. Calculate average input current

$$
I_{in,avg} = \frac{P_{in}}{V_{in,min}} = \frac{6W}{24V} = 0.25A
$$
## 3. Calculate $I_{pk}$ of primary side

![[Pasted image 20250811195241.png]]
We integrate by calculating the area of the waveform (the 1/2 is due to the waveform being a triangle):
$$
\begin{align}
I_{in,avg} &= \frac{1}{T_p} \int_0^{T_p} I(t) \, dt \\
&= \cfrac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot I_{pk} \cdot D \cdot T_p}{T_p} \\
&= 0.5 \cdot D \cdot I_{pk}
\end{align}
$$

We assumed duty cycle (D) = 0.5. Therefore,
$$
I_{pk} = 4 \cdot I_{in,avg} = 1A
$$
![[Pasted image 20250811195714.png]]

## 4. Calculate primary inductance

$$
V = L \frac{di}{dt}
$$Rearranging gives:
$$
L_{pri} = V \frac{dt}{di} = V_{in,min} \frac{T_{on}}{I_{pk}}
$$
$T_{on}$ can be found by multiplying duty cycle and period (1/freq)
$$
T_{on} = \frac{D}{\text{frequency}} = \frac{0.5}{100 \text{kHz}} = 5 \mu s
$$
Therefore,
$$
L_{pri} = \frac{24V \cdot 5 \mu s}{1A} = 120 \mu H
$$
## 5. Calculate turns ratio of flyback transformer

Recall the flyback diagram, the turns ratio is defined as 1:N. Therefore, $N_{pri} = 1$.
$$
N_r \text{(turns ratio)} = \frac{N_{sec}}{N_{pri}} = N_{sec}
$$
$$
V_o = N_r \cdot V_{in}
$$
Rearranging gives:
$$
N_{sec} = \frac{V_o}{V_{in}}
$$
