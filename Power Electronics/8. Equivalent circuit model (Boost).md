# Applying the transformer model

After drawing the 2 circuits, we can make it into a transformer model.

![[Pasted image 20250731195854.png]]

Verifying the model:
1. The current is "going into the dot" on the left hand side and "going out from the dot" on the right hand side. Which correlates to the 2 circuit we have drawn

## Output voltage (Secondary side)

"Pushing" the voltage source and the resistance to the secondary (right) side:

![[Pasted image 20250731200016.png]]

The solution is then solved using a simple voltage divider equation:
$$
V = \frac{V_g}{D'} \frac{R}{R + \frac{R_L}{D'^2}} = \frac{V_g}{D'} \frac{R}{1 + \frac{R_L}{D'^2 R}}
$$
## Inductor current (Primary side)

The load resistance $R$ is "pushed" to the primary side (left).
![[Pasted image 20250731200523.png]]

The solution is:
$$
I = \frac{V_g}{D'^2 R + R_L} = \frac{V_g}{RD'^2} \frac{1}{1 + \frac{R_L}{D'^2 R}}
$$
## Efficiency

From the image above (image in inductor current session) we know that:
$$
\begin{align}
P_{in} &= (V_g)(I) \\
P_{out} &= (V)(D'I)
\end{align}
$$
Efficiency is calculated by:
$$
\eta = \frac{P_{in}}{P_{out}} = \frac{(V_g)(I)}{(V)(D'I)} = \frac{V}{V_g}D'
$$
In the output voltage session (same page, above), we derived:
$$
V = \frac{V_g}{D'} \frac{R}{1 + \frac{R_L}{D'^2 R}}
$$
By rearranging we can then get the expression matching the efficiency:
$$
\eta = \frac{V}{V_g} D' = \frac{R}{1 + \frac{R_L}{D'^2 R}}
$$
