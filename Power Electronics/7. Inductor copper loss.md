## Switch in Position 1

Inductor current + capacitor voltage:
$$
\begin{align}
v_L(t) &= V_g - i(t) R_L \\
i_c(t) &= -v(t)/R
\end{align}
$$
Using the small ripple approximation:
$$
\begin{align}
v_L(t) &= V_g - I R_L \\
i_c(t) &= -V/R
\end{align}
$$
## Switch in Position 2

$$
\begin{align}
v_L(t) &= V_g - i(t) R_L -v(t) \\
i_c(t) &= i(t)-v(t)/R
\end{align}
$$
Using the small ripple approximation:
$$
\begin{align}
v_L(t) &= V_g - I R_L -V \\
i_c(t) &= I - V/R
\end{align}
$$
## Inductor voltage and capacitor current

We can now plot the waveforms of both the inductor voltage and capacitor current

![[Pasted image 20250731192734.png]]

Using the volt-second balance (avg inductor voltage = 0)
$$
\begin{align}
\langle v_L(t) \rangle &= D(V_g - IR_L) + D' (V_g - I R_L - V) \\
0 &= V_g - IR_L - D'V
\end{align}
$$
Using the capacitor charge balance (avg capacitor current = 0)
$$
\begin{align}
\langle i_c(t) \rangle &= D(-V/R) + D' (I - V/R) \\
0 &= D'I - V/R
\end{align}
$$
Solving the 2 equations gives:
$$
\frac{V}{V_g} = \frac{1}{D'} \frac{1}{1+\frac{R_L}{D'^2 R}}
$$
This equation is rearranged in a way that:
1. The first term describes the ideal case when there is no loss
2. The second term describes how the inductor resistance affects the output voltage
3. The second term is in the form $1/(1+x)$. As $R_L$ increases, the denominator increases and the output voltage will become small.
